Classify the whole visible chart by its geometric shape. Head and Shoulders has three peaks: the middle peak is highest, the outer peaks are similar, and the two intervening troughs form an approximately horizontal neckline. It completes when price crosses below that neckline. Inverse Head and Shoulders reverses the prices: three troughs, the middle deepest, then a break above the neckline connecting the two intervening peaks.

Double Top has two similar peaks separated by a substantial trough, followed by a break below the trough. Double Bottom has two similar troughs separated by a substantial peak, followed by a break above that peak. In these synthetic definitions, paired shoulders or peaks differ by at most three percent of the construction's price amplitude.

Triangle combines symmetric, ascending, and descending triangles. Alternating upper and lower pivots converge: both boundaries narrow, or one is horizontal while the other approaches it. Price must break outside the narrowing formation. Bull Flag begins with a steep upward pole, pauses in a smaller downward-sloping, approximately parallel channel, then breaks upward above the flag and pole high.

Choose None when none of these completed whole-window formations fits. A monotonic straight or gently curved path alone is None. Noise can make the intended geometry uncertain; consider the major pivots, not every candle wiggle. Short margins precede and follow the formation, and the breakout stays visible at the right. Horizontal mirroring reverses time; it does not exchange a top with a bottom. These labels describe visible structure, not a prediction of future returns.
