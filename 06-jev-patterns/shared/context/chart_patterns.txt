Classify the whole visible chart by its completed geometric structure. Head and Shoulders has three principal peaks: the middle peak is highest, the outer peaks are similar, and the intervening troughs form an approximately horizontal neckline. It completes when price crosses below that neckline. Inverse Head and Shoulders has three principal troughs, the middle deepest, followed by a break above the neckline connecting the two intervening peaks.

Double Top has two comparable peaks separated by a substantial trough, followed by a break below that trough. Double Bottom has two comparable troughs separated by a substantial peak, followed by a break above it. Peak spacing, formation height, modest shoulder asymmetry, and neckline tilt can vary; exact symmetry is unnecessary.

Triangle combines symmetric, ascending, and descending triangles. Alternating upper and lower pivots converge: both boundaries narrow, or one is approximately horizontal while the other approaches it. Completion requires price to exit the narrowing formation. Bull Flag begins with a steep upward pole, consolidates in a smaller downward-sloping, approximately parallel channel, then breaks upward above the flag and pole high.

Choose None when no completed whole-window formation fits. Random walks, choppy ranges, irregular pullbacks, incomplete formations, unequal reversals, and diverging or rising consolidations can be None. Trend direction alone is insufficient. Ignore small incidental fluctuations when identifying principal pivots. The margins, price level, volatility, bar count, and neutral volume bars are not class clues. Strong added noise can obscure the intended structure. Horizontal mirroring reverses time rather than exchanging tops and bottoms. Labels describe structure, not future returns.
