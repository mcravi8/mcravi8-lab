Chart-formation evidence: inspect the upper panel's broader structure. A completed head-and-shoulders top has three peaks, the central peak highest and the outer peaks approximately comparable, after an advance. A break below the neckline joining the intervening lows supports a downward hypothesis. Its inverted form after a decline has a deepest central trough and a break above the intervening highs, supporting an upward hypothesis. A double top has two comparable highs separated by a substantial low; a break below that intervening support supports Down. A double bottom reverses the geometry and supports Up after resistance is broken. Triangles have narrowing upper and lower boundaries. A symmetrical triangle alone supplies no direction; use an already visible breakout. Ascending or descending geometry is only a conditional clue, not a completed breakout. A bull flag follows a sharp advance with a smaller countertrend, roughly parallel consolidation; a visible upward break supports continuation. A bear flag reverses these directions. Incomplete, old or failed formations supply weaker evidence. A breakout that happened well before the final bar need not predict another move. Do not invent a named formation in an irregular chart. Evaluate the move still plausible over the next five sessions, rather than merely naming a pattern or repeating the historical trend.
