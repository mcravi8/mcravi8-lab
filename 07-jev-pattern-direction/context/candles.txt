Candlestick evidence: use the lower panel to inspect the most recent candles and their preceding trend. Body is the distance between open and close; wicks extend to the high and low. A hammer has a small body, a long lower wick and a small upper wick. After a decline it suggests rejection of lower prices and possible upward reversal. A shooting star has a small body, long upper wick and small lower wick; after a rise it suggests possible downward reversal. Bullish engulfing means a green body encloses the preceding red body; after a decline it supports an upward hypothesis. Bearish engulfing is the opposite and supports a downward hypothesis after a rise. A morning star has a long red candle, a small middle body, and a green recovery into the first body; an evening star reverses these colors and direction. A doji has a very small body and indicates uncertainty, not automatically a Flat five-session return. Treat these as uncertain directional clues. Check trend, nearby support/resistance and any confirmation already visible. Never assume that unseen future candles confirm a reversal.
